# The Constitution of the State of Texas

**Lone Star Edition**

## Preamble

Texas belongs to the people who live in it. We, the citizens of Texas, write this Constitution so that our community stays free, our leaders stay answerable, and every voice in the state carries weight. No office here is above the law, and no citizen is beneath its protection.

This Constitution is the highest law of Texas. Anything that conflicts with it has no force.

---

## Article 1: The Rights of Texans

**1.1 Equal standing.** Every Texan stands equal before the law, whatever their background, beliefs, or role in the community.

**1.2 Free voices.** Texans may speak, write, publish, worship (or not), organize, and protest peacefully. Government may not punish anyone for criticizing it.

**1.3 Fair treatment.** No one may be punished, jailed, or stripped of their property without a fair process. Anyone accused of wrongdoing:

- is presumed innocent until proven guilty;
- must be told what they are accused of;
- may bring a lawyer or advocate;
- may question the evidence and the witnesses against them;
- is entitled to a prompt, public hearing, and a jury of fellow citizens for serious charges.

No one may be punished twice for the same act, forced to testify against themselves, or given a cruel punishment.

**1.4 Privacy and property.** Homes, belongings, and personal information may not be searched or seized without a warrant from the Court, except when someone is in immediate danger. Property taken for public use must be paid for fairly.

**1.5 The right to be heard.** Every Texan may take a complaint to any public office and expect a written answer within fourteen days.

**1.6 The right to know.** Public records, budgets, votes, and court rulings are open to every citizen, except where privacy or safety demands otherwise.

**1.7 The vote.** Every citizen aged sixteen or over may vote by secret ballot, and may run for any office whose requirements they meet.

**1.8 Reasonable limits only.** A right may be limited only by a clear public law that is truly necessary. The protections in sections 1.3 and 1.7 can never be suspended, even in an emergency.

---

## Article 2: The Lone Star Legislature

**2.1 One chamber, seven seats.** Lawmaking power belongs to the Lone Star Legislature, a single chamber of seven members.

**2.2 How members are chosen.** Seats are filled at-large. Every citizen may vote for up to seven candidates, and the seven with the most votes win. Ties are settled by a runoff within three days.

**2.3 Length of term.** A term lasts eight months. There is no limit on how many times a member may run again.

**2.4 Who may serve.** A member must be a citizen aged eighteen or older, a resident of Texas for at least three months, and free of any serious criminal conviction. A member may not simultaneously be Governor, Sheriff, or a judge.

**2.5 Speaker.** At the start of each term, the members elect a Speaker from among themselves. The Speaker runs the meetings and votes only to break ties. The Speaker also holds the title Lieutenant Governor.

**2.6 Meetings.** The Legislature meets at least once a month, in open session. Four members make a quorum. Special meetings may be called by the Governor or by any three members.

**2.7 Town Hall Night.** Once each term, the Legislature and the Governor must hold an open public meeting where any citizen may ask questions and propose ideas.

**2.8 Making laws.** A bill may cover only one subject, which must appear in its title. It passes by a majority of all seven members. Every vote is recorded and published within seven days.

**2.9 The Governor's veto.** The Governor has seven days to sign a bill or send it back with written objections. If the Governor does neither, the bill becomes law. A returned bill becomes law anyway if five members vote for it again.

**2.10 Honest lawmaking.** Members must publicly list their financial interests and may not vote on matters that would personally enrich them.

**2.11 Filling empty seats.** The next-highest candidate from the last election takes a vacant seat. If none is available, a special election is held within forty-five days.

**2.12 Discipline.** Five members may expel a colleague for repeated absence, neglect of duty, or serious misconduct.

---

## Article 3: The Governor's Office

**3.1 Role.** The Governor leads the government of Texas, carries out the laws, and speaks for the state.

**3.2 Who may serve.** The Governor must be a citizen aged twenty-one or older, a resident for at least three months, and free of any serious criminal conviction.

**3.3 Election and term.** The Governor is elected by all citizens every six months. There is no limit on the number of terms a person may serve. If no candidate wins a majority, the top two face a runoff within one week.

**3.4 Powers.** The Governor may:

- issue orders needed to carry out the law (never to overrule it);
- nominate department heads and judges, who take office once a majority of the Legislature confirms them;
- propose the yearly budget;
- grant pardons, but only on the Court's recommendation.

**3.5 Duties.** The Governor must report to the Legislature at least monthly, and may not hold any other paid office.

**3.6 If the seat is empty.** If the Governor leaves, is removed, or is absent for thirty days, the Lieutenant Governor takes over. When the vacancy will last more than thirty days, a new election is held within fourteen days.

**3.7 Emergencies.** The Governor may declare an emergency for up to fourteen days when there is a disaster, an attack, or a serious threat. Only the Legislature may extend it. Emergency powers may never suspend the Legislature, cancel elections, or override section 1.8.

---

## Article 4: The Sheriff

**4.1 Election and term.** Citizens elect a Sheriff to an eight-month term, with no limit on the number of terms. The Sheriff must be a citizen aged eighteen or older with at least three months' residence.

**4.2 Duties.** The Sheriff keeps the peace, enforces the law, and carries out the Court's warrants and orders. The Sheriff may appoint deputies with the Governor's consent.

**4.3 Limits.** The Sheriff and deputies may not arrest anyone for lawful political activity. Any militia or guard raised by Texas answers to civilian authority and may never interfere with an election.

---

## Article 5: The Court of Texas

**5.1 Three judges.** The Court of Texas has three judges. They serve for as long as they conduct themselves properly.

**5.2 What the Court does.** The Court hears criminal cases, civil disputes, and questions about the meaning of this Constitution. It may strike down any law or official act that violates it.

**5.3 Choosing judges.** The Governor nominates, and five members of the Legislature must confirm. A judge must be a citizen aged twenty-five or older without a serious conviction.

**5.4 Independence.** No official may pressure a judge or interfere with a pending case. Rulings must be written with reasons and published.

**5.5 Citizen jurors.** Serious criminal trials are heard by a jury of citizens, with one judge presiding. A judge with a personal stake in a case must step aside and is replaced for that case by a citizen chosen by lot.

**5.6 Removing a judge.** Five members of the Legislature may remove a judge for serious misconduct after a public hearing.

---

## Article 6: The People's Power

**6.1 The Election Clerk.** The Legislature appoints an Election Clerk, who may be removed only by the Court. The Clerk keeps the Citizen Roll, runs all elections, counts the votes, and publishes the results. The Clerk may never run for office.

**6.2 Fair elections.** Elections are held on set dates by secret ballot, and any citizen may watch the counting. The Court settles election disputes within fourteen days.

**6.3 Citizen lawmaking.** If one-quarter of citizens sign a petition for a new law, the Legislature must either pass it or put it to a public vote within sixty days. A majority of voters decides.

**6.4 Citizen veto.** If one-quarter of citizens sign a petition within thirty days after a law passes, that law is paused until the voters decide whether to keep it.

**6.5 Recall.** Any citizen may start a recall of the Governor, a legislator, or the Sheriff, which goes to a public vote within one week once one-third of citizens sign the petition. The petition must state its reasons (unfitness, neglect of duty, or corruption) in 200 words or fewer. No petition may be filed in the first fourteen days of a term. If a majority votes to remove, the officer is out.

---

## Article 7: Accountability

**7.1 Impeachment.** Five members of the Legislature may bring written charges against the Governor, the Sheriff, or a judge. The Court tries the case, and two of the three judges must agree to convict. If a judge is on trial, the other two judges sit together with a citizen chosen by lot.

**7.2 Grounds.** Only serious crimes, bribery, corruption, incompetence, or repeated neglect of duty are grounds for impeachment.

**7.3 Penalty.** Conviction means removal from office. It does not prevent a separate criminal case.

**7.4 Yearly review.** Every year, the Legislature must review each government office to decide whether it is useful, fair, and worth its cost.

---

## Article 8: The Public Purse

**8.1 No spending without a law.** Taxes may be collected and money spent only as authorized by law. The Legislature passes the budget each year.

**8.2 Treasurer.** A Treasurer, nominated by the Governor and confirmed by the Legislature, keeps the accounts and posts them publicly every three months.

**8.3 Auditor.** The Legislature appoints an independent Auditor who is not part of the government. The Auditor checks the accounts at least once a year and reports publicly.

**8.4 Borrowing.** Texas may borrow only if five members of the Legislature approve.

---

## Article 9: The Texas Oath and Closing Provisions

**9.1 The Oath.** Every official must swear this oath on taking office:

> I promise to serve the people of Texas honestly, to uphold this Constitution, to treat every Texan fairly, and to leave my office when the people ask me to.

**9.2 Amendments.** An amendment may be proposed by five members of the Legislature or by a petition of one-quarter of citizens. It takes effect when a majority of citizens voting approve it, in a vote held at least thirty days after it is published.

**9.3 Untouchable core.** No amendment may remove the right to vote, free elections, the independence of the Court, or the protections in section 1.3.

**9.4 Ten-year review.** Every ten years the Legislature must hold a public review of this Constitution.

**9.5 Existing laws.** Laws already in force stay in force unless they conflict with this Constitution.

**9.6 If part fails.** If a court finds one part invalid, the rest remains in force.

**9.7 Taking effect.** This Constitution takes effect when a majority of citizens voting approve it.
