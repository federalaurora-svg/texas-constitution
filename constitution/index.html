    How the people hold power: citizens may write laws by petition (one-quarter of citizens), pause laws by petition (one-quarter), and remove officials by recall (one-third).

    Preamble

    Texas belongs to the people who live in it. We, the citizens of Texas, write this Constitution so that our community stays free, our leaders stay answerable, and every voice in the state carries weight. We believe that power is only borrowed from the people, that it must be used openly and fairly, and that it must be handed back when the people ask for it. No office here is above the law, and no citizen is beneath its protection.

    This Constitution is the highest law of Texas. Any law, order, or action that conflicts with it has no force.

    Article 1: The Rights of Texans

    1.1 Equal Standing

    Every Texan stands equal before the law, whatever their background, beliefs, or role in the community. No one receives special treatment because of who they are, who they know, or what office they hold.

    1.2 Free Voices

    Texans may speak, write, publish, and worship (or choose not to worship) as they see fit. Government may not punish anyone for criticizing it, and it may not favor or punish any religion or belief.

    1.3 Assembly and Association

    Texans may gather peacefully, form groups and political parties, join or leave any association, and petition the government for change. No one may be forced to join a group against their will.

    1.4 Fair Treatment

    No one may be punished, jailed, or stripped of their property without a fair process. That process must be written down in advance, public, and applied the same way to everyone.

    1.5 Rights of the Accused

    Anyone accused of wrongdoing:

    • is presumed innocent until proven guilty;
    • must be told, in plain language, what they are accused of;
    • may bring a lawyer or advocate, and the state shall help provide one if the accused cannot find one;
    • may question the evidence and the witnesses against them;
    • is entitled to a prompt, public hearing, and to a jury of fellow citizens for serious charges.

    No one may be punished twice for the same act, forced to testify against themselves, or given a cruel or degrading punishment.

    1.6 Privacy and Property

    Homes, belongings, and personal information may not be searched or seized without a warrant from the Court, except when someone is in immediate danger. Property taken for public use must be paid for fairly, and the owner may challenge the amount before the Court.

    1.7 The Right to Be Heard

    Every Texan may bring a complaint to any public office and expect a written answer within fourteen days. An office that cannot solve the problem must say so and point to the office that can.

    1.8 The Right to Know

    Public records, budgets, votes, and court rulings are open to every citizen, except where privacy or safety demands otherwise. An office that withholds a record must give its reasons in writing.

    1.9 The Vote

    Every citizen aged sixteen or over may vote by secret ballot, and may run for any office whose requirements they meet. No one may be prevented from voting because of their wealth, beliefs, or background.

    1.10 Equal Service

    Public offices must serve every Texan fairly and may not refuse, delay, or worsen service because of who someone is.

    1.11 Rights Not Listed

    This list is not complete. Rights not named here still belong to the people, and no part of this Constitution shall be read to take them away.

    1.12 Reasonable Limits Only

    A right may be limited only by a clear public law that is truly necessary and no broader than needed. The protections in sections 1.4, 1.5, and 1.9 can never be suspended, even in an emergency.

    Article 2: Territory and Boundaries

    2.1 The Territory of Texas

    The State of Texas consists of the land, waters, and structures within the boundaries set out in Schedule A of this Constitution.

    2.2 Describing the Boundaries

    The boundaries of Texas shall be described clearly in Schedule A, so that every citizen can tell where Texas begins and ends. Boundary descriptions shall be published and kept current.

    2.3 Changing the Boundaries

    The boundaries of Texas may be changed only by law. Five members of the Legislature must propose the change, a public hearing must be held, and a majority of citizens voting must approve it.

    2.4 The Capital

    The capital of Texas is Austin. The Legislature may move the capital by law if five members vote for it, and a majority of citizens voting approve.

    2.5 State Symbols

    The Lone Star flag is the flag of Texas. The Legislature may adopt other state symbols by law.

    Article 3: Citizenship and the Citizen Roll

    3.1 Who Is a Citizen

    A citizen is anyone listed on the Citizen Roll.

    3.2 Joining

    A person may join the Citizen Roll by being born in Texas, by being born to a citizen, or by naturalizing. To naturalize, a person must live in Texas for at least three months and be approved by a majority of the Legislature.

    3.3 The Citizen Roll

    The Election Clerk keeps the Citizen Roll. It must be accurate, kept up to date, and open for any citizen to check their own entry. Mistakes must be corrected within seven days of a citizen reporting them.

    3.4 Keeping Citizenship

    Citizenship may not be taken away, except by the Court, and only for fraud in obtaining it. A citizen who leaves Texas may return and rejoin the Roll without a waiting period.

    3.5 Duties of Citizens

    Citizens are encouraged to vote, to serve on juries when chosen, and to respect the rights of others. These duties may not be used as a reason to take away any right.

    3.6 Residency

    A person is a resident of Texas once they have lived in Texas continuously. Short absences do not break residency. A person who lives in a county or town for at least one month is a resident of that county or town.

    Article 4: The Lone Star Legislature

    4.1 One Chamber, Seven Seats

    Lawmaking power belongs to the Lone Star Legislature, a single chamber of seven members. No other body may make laws for the whole of Texas except as this Constitution allows.

    4.2 How Members Are Chosen

    Seats are filled at-large. Every citizen may vote for up to seven candidates, and the seven with the most votes win. Ties are settled by a runoff within three days.

    4.3 Length of Term

    A term lasts eight months. There is no limit on how many times a member may run again, because the people may remove any member they no longer trust.

    4.4 Who May Serve

    A member must be a citizen aged eighteen or older, a resident of Texas for at least three months, and free of any serious criminal conviction. A member may not simultaneously be Governor, Sheriff, or a judge. A member may run for another office and must resign their seat if they win it.

    4.5 Speaker

    At the start of each term, the members elect a Speaker from among themselves. The Speaker runs the meetings and votes only to break ties. The Speaker also holds the title Lieutenant Governor.

    4.6 Meetings

    The Legislature meets at least once a month, in open session. Special meetings may be called by the Governor or by any three members. At a meeting called by the Governor, only the topics the Governor names may be taken up.

    4.7 Quorum and Votes

    Four members make a quorum. A decision needs a majority of the members present, unless this Constitution says otherwise. A smaller group may adjourn the meeting and summon absent members.

    4.8 Making Laws

    A bill may cover only one subject, which must appear in its title. It becomes law when a majority of all seven members vote for it and the Governor signs it (or the veto is overridden). Every vote is recorded and published within seven days.

    4.9 The Governor's Veto

    The Governor has seven days to sign a bill or send it back with written objections. If the Governor does neither, the bill becomes law. A returned bill becomes law anyway if five members vote for it again.

    4.10 Town Hall Night

    Once each term, the Legislature and the Governor must hold an open public meeting where any citizen may ask questions and propose ideas. Questions that cannot be answered on the night must be answered in writing within fourteen days.

    4.11 Open Records

    Minutes, bills, and the record of every vote must be published for the public within seven days. The Legislature may meet in private only to protect someone's safety or privacy, and only if five members agree.

    4.12 Honest Lawmaking

    Members must publicly list their financial interests and may not vote on matters that would personally enrich them or their families.

    4.13 Filling Empty Seats

    The next-highest candidate from the last election takes a vacant seat. If none is available, a special election is held within forty-five days. If fewer than thirty days remain in the term, the Governor may fill the seat by appointment, confirmed by a majority of the Legislature.

    4.14 Discipline

    Five members may expel a colleague for repeated absence, neglect of duty, or serious misconduct. The member must be given written charges and a chance to respond before the vote.

    Article 5: The Governor and the Lieutenant Governor

    5.1 Role

    The Governor leads the government of Texas, carries out the laws, and speaks for the state. The Governor serves the whole community, not a party or a group.

    5.2 Who May Serve

    The Governor must be a citizen aged twenty-one or older, a resident for at least three months, and free of any serious criminal conviction.

    5.3 Election and Term

    The Governor is elected by all citizens every six months. There is no limit on the number of terms a person may serve. If no candidate wins a majority, the top two face a runoff within one week.

    5.4 Powers

    The Governor may:

    • issue orders needed to carry out the law;
    • nominate department heads and judges, who take office once a majority of the Legislature confirms them (five members for judges);
    • propose the yearly budget;
    • call the Legislature into special session;
    • represent Texas to outsiders and agree to arrangements with other communities, subject to approval by a majority of the Legislature.

    5.5 Limits on Power

    An order may never overrule a law or this Constitution. The Governor may not hold any other paid office and may not use public resources for personal gain.

    5.6 Monthly Report

    The Governor must report to the Legislature at least monthly on what the government has done and what it plans to do. The report must be published for the public.

    5.7 The Lieutenant Governor

    The Speaker of the Legislature is the Lieutenant Governor. The Lieutenant Governor acts as Governor whenever the Governor is temporarily away, and carries out any duties the Governor assigns.

    5.8 If the Seat Is Empty

    If the Governor resigns, dies, is removed, or is absent for thirty days, the Lieutenant Governor becomes Governor. If the vacancy will last longer than thirty days, a new election is held within fourteen days.

    5.9 Pardons

    The Governor may grant pardons and reduce sentences, but only on the Court's recommendation. Every pardon must be published with the reasons for it.

    5.10 Handing Over

    An outgoing Governor must hand over all records and duties to the new Governor within three days of the election result.

    Article 6: Departments and Public Servants

    6.1 Departments

    The Governor may propose departments to carry out the work of government. A department exists only once a majority of the Legislature approves it, and the law that creates it must say what it does.

    6.2 Department Heads

    Each department has one head, nominated by the Governor and confirmed by a majority of the Legislature. A department head serves at the Governor's pleasure and may be dismissed at any time, with the reason reported to the Legislature.

    6.3 Fair Hiring

    Public servants shall be chosen for their ability and shall serve all Texans equally, regardless of political party. No one may be hired, fired, or promoted because of their politics.

    6.4 Yearly Review

    Every year, the Legislature must review each government office to decide whether it is useful, fair, and worth its cost. An office that fails its review may be reformed or closed by law.

    6.5 Public Reports

    Each department must publish a short report on its work at least once a year, including what it spent and what it achieved.

    Article 7: The Sheriff and Public Safety

    7.1 Election and Term

    Citizens elect a Sheriff every eight months. There is no limit on the number of terms a person may serve.

    7.2 Who May Serve

    The Sheriff must be a citizen aged eighteen or older with at least three months' residence and no serious criminal conviction.

    7.3 Duties

    The Sheriff keeps the peace across Texas, enforces the law, and carries out the Court's warrants and orders. The Sheriff must answer to the Court and the Legislature for how they use their power.

    7.4 Deputies

    The Sheriff may appoint deputies with the Governor's consent. Deputies serve at the Sheriff's pleasure and may be assigned to work in any county or town.

    7.5 Limits

    The Sheriff and deputies may not arrest anyone for lawful political activity, may not use more force than necessary, and are bound by every right in Article 1.

    7.6 Civilian Control

    Any militia or guard raised by Texas answers to civilian authority and may never interfere with an election or be used against peaceful citizens.

    Article 8: The Court of Texas

    8.1 Three Judges

    The Court of Texas has three judges. They serve for as long as they conduct themselves properly.

    8.2 What the Court Does

    The Court hears criminal cases, civil disputes, and questions about the meaning of this Constitution. It may strike down any law or official act that violates it.

    8.3 Choosing Judges

    The Governor nominates, and five members of the Legislature must confirm. The Governor may ask a panel of citizens to review nominees before a vote.

    8.4 Who May Serve

    A judge must be a citizen aged twenty-five or older without a serious criminal conviction. Legal training is preferred, but not required.

    8.5 Independence

    No official may pressure a judge or interfere with a pending case. A judge's pay and position may not be threatened to influence a ruling.

    8.6 Citizen Jurors

    Serious criminal trials are heard by a jury of citizens, with one judge presiding. A judge with a personal stake in a case must step aside and is replaced for that case by a citizen chosen by lot.

    8.7 Warrants and Writs

    The Court may issue warrants and writs. Anyone served with one must follow it, though they may appeal it to the full Court.

    8.8 Removing a Judge

    Five members of the Legislature may remove a judge for serious misconduct after a public hearing.

    8.9 Public Rulings

    Every ruling must be written, give its reasons, and be published.

    Article 9: Local Government

    9.1 Counties and Towns

    Texas is divided into counties, and counties may contain towns. Counties and towns exist so that decisions are made as close to the people affected as possible. The counties and towns of Texas are listed in Schedules B and C.

    9.2 Creating and Changing Local Governments

    The Legislature may create, merge, divide, or abolish a county or town by law. Five members must vote for the change, a public hearing must be held, and a majority of the citizens living in the affected area must approve it.

    9.3 The County Council

    Each county is governed by a County Council of three members, elected at-large by the citizens of that county for eight-month terms. There is no limit on the number of terms. A council member must be a citizen aged eighteen or older who has lived in the county for at least one month. The council elects its own chair, meets at least once a month in open session, and keeps public minutes.

    9.4 The County Prosecutor

    Each county elects a County Prosecutor for an eight-month term. The Prosecutor brings criminal charges on behalf of the people of the county and must act fairly, independent of the Governor and the County Council. The Prosecutor must give written reasons whenever they decline to prosecute a reported serious crime.

    9.5 Towns

    A town is governed by a Mayor and a Town Council of three members, all elected at-large by the citizens of that town for eight-month terms. The Mayor leads the town government and carries out its ordinances. The council passes ordinances, approves the town budget, and oversees the Mayor.

    9.6 Local Ordinances

    A county or town may make ordinances on local matters, such as markets, public spaces, local services, and local events, so long as they do not conflict with state law or this Constitution. Every ordinance must be published and filed with the Speaker of the Legislature, and takes effect on the date it states or seven days after filing, whichever is later.

    9.7 Charters

    A county or town may adopt a charter that sets out how its government works, such as the number of council members or how they are chosen. A charter takes effect when three-fifths of the local citizens voting approve it and five members of the Legislature agree it follows this Constitution. A charter may not remove the office of County Prosecutor, change the length of any term set by this Constitution, or take away any right in Article 1.

    9.8 Local Powers of the People

    Citizens of a county or town may use the powers in Article 11 against their local government. The numbers needed are the same fractions of the local citizens. For example, one-third of the citizens of a county may start a recall of its council members.

    9.9 Local Money

    A county or town may collect only the taxes and fees authorized by state law or by its charter. It may borrow only if a majority of its citizens voting approve and five members of the Legislature agree. Every county and town must publish its budget and accounts every three months.

    9.10 Failed Local Governments

    If a county council or town council has not met for thirty days, or if it cannot form a quorum, it is declared defunct by the Court. The Legislature then performs its duties, and the Election Clerk must hold a new local election within fourteen days. The new officials take over when the election result is published.

    9.11 Disputes Between Governments

    Disputes between the state, a county, and a town are settled by the Court. Until the Court rules, state law prevails over local ordinances, and a charter prevails over a local ordinance.

    Article 10: Elections

    10.1 The Election Clerk

    The Legislature appoints an Election Clerk, who may be removed only by the Court. The Clerk keeps the Citizen Roll, runs all state and local elections, counts the votes, and publishes the results. The Clerk may never run for office.

    10.2 Fair Elections

    Elections are held on set dates by secret ballot, and any citizen may watch the voting and the counting. No one may be kept away from the ballot by threats, tricks, or unreasonable rules.

    10.3 Candidates

    Any citizen who meets the requirements of an office may run for it. No one may be kept off the ballot except for failing those requirements.

    10.4 Counting and Results

    The Clerk must count the votes openly and publish the full results within three days of the vote. Ballots and records must be kept safe until any dispute is settled.

    10.5 Disputes

    The Court settles election disputes within fourteen days.

    10.6 Campaign Fairness

    No one may buy votes or threaten voters. Candidates must publicly disclose large gifts and donations.

    10.7 Election Calendar

    The Governor is elected every six months. Legislators, the Sheriff, and all county and town officials are elected every eight months. The Clerk must announce all election dates at least fourteen days ahead.

    Article 11: Power of the People

    11.1 Citizen Lawmaking

    If one-quarter of citizens sign a petition for a new law, the Legislature must either pass it or put it to a public vote within sixty days. A majority of voters decides.

    11.2 Citizen Veto

    If one-quarter of citizens sign a petition within thirty days after a law passes, that law is paused until the voters decide whether to keep it.

    11.3 Recall

    The Governor, any legislator, and the Sheriff may be recalled. A recall goes to a public vote within one week once one-third of citizens sign the petition.

    11.4 Rules for Recall

    The petition must state its reasons (unfitness, incompetence, neglect of duty, or corruption) in 200 words or fewer. No recall petition may be filed in the first fourteen days of a term. If a majority votes to remove, the officer is out.

    11.5 Checking Petitions

    The Election Clerk must verify the signatures on any petition within seven days and publish the result.

    11.6 Limits

    Petitions may not be used to create courts or to change the untouchable core in section 15.3.

    Article 12: Accountability and Ethics

    12.1 Impeachment

    Five members of the Legislature may bring written charges against the Governor, the Sheriff, a county prosecutor, or a judge.

    12.2 Grounds

    Only serious crimes, bribery, corruption, incompetence, or repeated neglect of duty are grounds for impeachment.

    12.3 Trial

    The Court tries the case, and two of the three judges must agree to convict. If a judge is on trial, the other two judges sit together with a citizen chosen by lot.

    12.4 Penalty

    Conviction means removal from office. It does not prevent a separate criminal case.

    12.5 Suspension

    The Legislature may suspend an accused official for up to seven days while a trial is arranged.

    12.6 Conflicts of Interest

    Officials must step away from any decision in which they have a personal or financial stake.

    12.7 Gifts

    Officials may not accept gifts meant to influence their decisions.

    12.8 Whistleblowers

    No one may be punished for truthfully reporting wrongdoing by a public official.

    Article 13: The Public Purse

    13.1 No Spending Without a Law

    Taxes may be collected and money spent only as authorized by law.

    13.2 The Yearly Budget

    The Governor proposes a budget each year, and the Legislature must pass it. The budget must be published before the vote so that citizens can read it.

    13.3 Treasurer

    A Treasurer, nominated by the Governor and confirmed by the Legislature, keeps the accounts and posts them publicly every three months.

    13.4 Auditor

    The Legislature appoints an independent Auditor who is not part of the government. The Auditor checks the accounts at least once a year and reports publicly.

    13.5 Borrowing

    Texas may borrow only if five members of the Legislature approve, and only if the debt can reasonably be repaid.

    13.6 Public Resources

    Land, water, and natural resources belong to all Texans and shall be used for the common good.

    Article 14: Emergencies

    14.1 Declaring an Emergency

    The Governor may declare an emergency when there is a disaster, an attack, or another serious threat to the people of Texas. The declaration must say what the threat is.

    14.2 Length

    An emergency lasts up to fourteen days. Only the Legislature may extend it.

    14.3 Limits

    Emergency powers may never suspend the Legislature, cancel elections, or override section 1.12.

    14.4 Court Review

    The Court may review any emergency order and cancel it if it goes beyond what the emergency requires.

    14.5 Ending an Emergency

    An emergency ends when the danger ends, when it runs out, or when the Legislature ends it.

    Article 15: Amendments and General Provisions

    15.1 Proposing an Amendment

    An amendment may be proposed by five members of the Legislature or by a petition of one-quarter of citizens.

    15.2 Approving an Amendment

    An amendment takes effect when a majority of citizens voting approve it, in a vote held at least thirty days after it is published.

    15.3 Untouchable Core

    No amendment may remove the right to vote, free elections, the independence of the Court, or the protections in sections 1.4 and 1.5.

    15.4 Ten-Year Review

    Every ten years the Legislature must hold a public review of this Constitution.

    15.5 Existing Laws

    Laws already in force stay in force unless they conflict with this Constitution.

    15.6 If Part Fails

    If a court finds one part invalid, the rest remains in force.

    15.7 Highest Law

    This Constitution outranks every other law, order, charter, and policy in Texas.

    15.8 Plain Reading

    This Constitution shall be read in its plain, ordinary meaning, in favor of the rights of the people.

    15.9 Taking Effect

    This Constitution takes effect when a majority of citizens voting approve it.

    The Texas Oath

    Every official, at every level of government, must swear this oath on taking office:

    I promise to serve the people of Texas honestly, to uphold this Constitution, to treat every Texan fairly, and to leave my office when the people ask me to.
